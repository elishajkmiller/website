About me

I'm Elisha, a technologist and systems builder in Joplin, MO. I build operational frameworks for organizations: systems that walk people through their work, and the software that runs them.

I try to reduce suffering. I go from department to department and build tools and operating frameworks that guide people through their jobs, step by step. The software this field is stuck with is expensive and built around billing, not around the people doing the work, so I'm building Robin, an open-source record system for human services nonprofits. It's the most real thing I've made. I also run the website and operations for POW417, a 48-hour human-only filmmaking competition in Springfield, MO. There is no AI-generated content. Forty-something people show up and make something in two days, and it means something because people made it.

I'm obsessed with efficiency and with data visualization. I've built automatic billing and route optimizers that pull live data, and I like turning numbers into pictures that people can read at a glance.

On my own time I make hardware and firmware. I wrote a UI for the LilyGO T-Deck Pro on top of Meshtastic, and firmware for a Watchy that gets my texts from it. I'm also converting a kei truck to electric with a battery I'm building from scratch.

I like looking at how things on the internet actually behave. I read headers, requests and DNS records, then write down what I find. A couple of those write-ups are on the blog.

I run open AI models on my own computers, and I train, fine-tune and retrain them there. No hosted AI services. More on that in my data policy.

The rest is the Fediverse, self-hosting everything I can, and music in REAPER on Linux Mint. My card has the ways to reach me.

I don't have a degree. I learned by building things for people I know.
